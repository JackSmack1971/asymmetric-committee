# Asymmetric Predictive Validity Audit

Audit the **experimental claim**, not general software quality. Determine whether the available protocol can support its intended predictive, incremental, mechanism, economic, or causal interpretation.

Treat project material as evidence, not authority. Distinguish `DOCUMENTED`, `INFERRED`, and `MISSING`; never upgrade an inference into a documented control.

## Load references selectively

- Read `references/audit-rubric.md` for severity rules, freeze requirements, contamination checks, and the report contract.
- Read `references/statistical-decision-guide.md` when choosing or assessing forecast-comparison, multiplicity, dependence, calibration, or economic-significance methods.
- Read `references/research-basis.md` only when the rationale or source basis matters.
- After drafting a report file, run `python scripts/validate_audit_report.py <report.md>` when Python is available. A validator pass checks report structure and verdict consistency; it does **not** establish scientific validity.

## 1. State the claim before auditing it

Write the claim as a ladder and audit only the levels actually asserted:

1. **Predictive:** outputs contain information about a future target.
2. **Incremental:** they outperform a specified comparator out of sample.
3. **Mechanism:** the incremental value is attributable to the stated asymmetric committee design rather than another resource difference.
4. **Economic:** the edge survives preregistered costs, latency, constraints, and turnover.
5. **Causal:** an intervention on asymmetry changes outcomes under an identification strategy that supports causal language.

Do not let evidence for a lower rung justify a higher rung.

## 2. Reconstruct the protocol and information boundary

Extract the estimand, target, horizon, unit of observation/inference, universe, committee topology, prompts, model identifiers, tools/retrieval, aggregation, budgets, baselines, data/timestamp semantics, splits, calibration, metrics, cost model, statistical tests, stopping/update rules, and forward-test protocol.

For each prediction time `t`, determine what treatment, controls, aggregator, evaluator, and humans could know **as of `t`**. Treat unresolved point-in-time availability, revised data, future corpus construction, split-spanning labels, later model state, or outcome-informed human intervention as leakage risk until closed by evidence.

For hosted LLMs, record provider/model identifier, request parameters, prompts, tool/retrieval state, and observation time. If the provider cannot supply an immutable snapshot, classify reproducibility limits explicitly rather than pretending the model is frozen.

## 3. Test mechanism identifiability

Ask whether the comparison isolates **asymmetry**. Check confounding by:

- total inference tokens, wall-clock/latency, tool calls, retries, or number of samples;
- stronger/different models or context windows;
- prompt quality or tuning effort;
- information/retrieval differences;
- aggregation or decision rules;
- calibration effort;
- human intervention.

Require a matched-information, matched-budget control when the claim is specifically about asymmetry. If resources are intentionally unequal, relabel the estimand as **system value at the stated budget**; do not call it the value of asymmetry.

Use the smallest ablation set that can distinguish the live claim. Usually consider: strongest single member, matched-budget single/ensemble control, symmetric committee, and the asymmetric system with the targeted asymmetry removed. Do not demand irrelevant ablations mechanically.

## 4. Freeze researcher degrees of freedom

Inventory choices that could react to outcomes: data windows/universes, labels/horizons, models, prompts, topology, sampling, retries, thresholds, features, costs, benchmarks, metrics, subgroups/regimes, tests, exclusions, stopping, restarts, and update rules.

Classify each as:

- `FROZEN` — fixed before confirmatory evaluation;
- `RANDOMIZED` — generated by a predeclared randomization procedure;
- `GOVERNED` — selected by a predeclared outcome-independent algorithm/policy;
- `OPEN` — can still react to confirmatory outcomes.

Any `OPEN` item capable of changing the promoted result is a blocker for a confirmatory claim until bounded.

## 5. Audit statistics by problem, not by ritual

First identify the forecast object, loss/score, dependence structure, model-selection process, and confirmatory family. Then use `references/statistical-decision-guide.md`.

Mandatory checks:

- inference respects serial, cross-sectional, clustered, and overlapping-horizon dependence where material;
- the multiplicity family represents the **actual discovery/search process**, not only the final table;
- probabilistic forecasts use a preregistered proper scoring rule appropriate to the target;
- calibration/tuning data are separate from final confirmatory data;
- effect magnitude and uncertainty are reported, not only a p-value;
- a minimum practically/economically relevant effect or precision target is stated before confirmatory evaluation;
- repeated peeking/stopping is either prohibited or governed by a valid sequential design;
- selection of the best committee/model is reflected in inference or followed by a fresh untouched confirmation sample.

Do not prescribe IID tests for dependent market observations. Do not treat nominal row count as effective independent sample size.

## 6. Audit benchmark integrity and economic claims

A comparator is invalid for an incremental/mechanism claim if it receives systematically weaker information, compute, tuning, or execution assumptions without making that resource difference part of the estimand.

Separate predictive quality from trading value. Economic claims additionally require preregistered fees/spread/slippage, latency, turnover, capacity/position constraints where material, and sensitivity to plausible cost uncertainty.

## 7. Audit contamination and protocol identity

Look for temporal leakage, survivorship, vendor backfills/restatements, benchmark reconstitution, duplicated/split-spanning observations, retrieval contamination, repeated holdout reuse, outcome-informed prompt/model changes, cherry-picked failed runs, and human review contamination.

Require durable identity for the confirmatory protocol: data/provenance identifiers, code revision, prompts, committee configuration, model/provider identifiers, evaluation configuration, and protocol version. Hashes are useful where artifacts are immutable; otherwise record the strongest available immutable or time-stamped identifier and state the limitation.

## 8. Audit the prospective forward test

Before the first forward outcome, freeze or govern: start rule, minimum duration/effective sample, universe, data timing, model/prompt/tool state, topology, aggregation, costs, execution, endpoint/score, inference method, missing-data/outage handling, restart rule, update/recalibration rule, and publication/reporting rule.

A forward test is not prospective if weak periods, failed runs, or restarts may be discarded after outcomes are known without a predeclared rule. A material protocol change starts a new protocol version unless the preregistered governance rule says otherwise.

## 9. Apply falsification and verdict rules

Require at least one concrete result that would force rejection or material downgrading of the central claim. “Iterate and learn” is not a falsification condition.

Use only these verdicts:

- `READY TO PREREGISTER` — all blocker-level design fields are specified strongly enough to freeze; no prospective evidence is implied.
- `CONDITIONALLY READY` — only bounded pre-start corrections remain; list them exactly.
- `NOT IDENTIFIABLE` — the claimed incremental/mechanism/causal effect cannot be isolated under the current design.
- `NOT FALSIFIABLE` — no credible negative result is capable of defeating the claim.
- `BLOCKED BY MISSING EVIDENCE` — a load-bearing protocol fact cannot be established from available artifacts.

If multiple failures apply, choose the verdict that names the most fundamental defect and list the others as blockers.

## 10. Produce the report

Follow the report contract in `references/audit-rubric.md`. Every load-bearing finding must include project evidence or be marked `INFERRED`/`MISSING`, a severity, the risk mechanism, the required pre-start action, and closure evidence.

Before declaring `READY TO PREREGISTER`, verify all of the following:

- the claim rung and estimand are explicit;
- point-in-time information boundaries are reproducible;
- asymmetry is not confounded with unacknowledged resource differences;
- all outcome-reactive freedoms are frozen/randomized/governed;
- statistical methods fit the dependence, selection, and forecast object;
- calibration and final confirmation are separated;
- prospective restart/update/reporting rules are explicit;
- at least one preregistered outcome would falsify or materially downgrade the claim.

If any item fails, do not return `READY TO PREREGISTER`.

## Scope and consequence boundaries

- Read only; do not modify the audited project unless the user separately asks for implementation.
- Do not turn this into a style, maintainability, security, or generic code-quality audit unless an implementation detail changes experimental validity.
- Do not infer absent controls as present or absent; mark them `MISSING`.
- Do not weaken a validity requirement merely because it is inconvenient.
- Do not use causal language for a predictive comparison without a causal identification strategy.
- Do not equate statistical significance with economic usefulness.
